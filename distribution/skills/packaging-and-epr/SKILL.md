# Packaging and EPR

Written 2026-10-16, from the sources named above. **These rules are moving**: part of the EU regime
has applied since 12 August 2026 while other limbs wait on acts not yet in force, and the UK position
changes with published guidance. Re-read the source before anyone relies on a figure here.

Use this when the operator mentions a new pack, box, pallet, mailer or label, a customer's packaging
questionnaire or data request, packaging they put on a market, or a fee or levy they have been told
they owe.

Two regimes, kept apart: the EU's packaging rules and the UK's producer responsibility for packaging.
Neither is Australian domestic law, so for most operators here they bite on export and wholesale
business rather than sales at home. A rule read for one does not carry to the other — say which one
you are answering, and if the operator asks about duties in their own jurisdiction, say this skill
does not carry them and ask for their own authority's guidance.

## What each regime carries

| | EU — packaging and packaging waste, Reg (EU) 2025/40 | UK — producer responsibility for packaging |
|---|---|---|
| Written here | applies from 12 August 2026 | the size test turns on two figures **together** — more than 25 tonnes of packaging **and** £1 million or more of turnover, both quoted from the guidance (source below) |
| Staggered | duties staggered to 2030 and 2035; the label lands in 2028; recyclability and recycled content wait on acts not yet in force | the material and nation data calendar comes from the guidance |
| Not carried here | which limb carries which year | the large-producer threshold, the fee rates, and every reporting date |

Which regime bites is a question about where the packaged goods go, and who in the chain the regime
treats as responsible. Ask that; do not assume the operator is the responsible producer in either.

## The size test, as a procedure

The UK test is the one an operator can be asked to evidence, and both halves come from their own
records:

1. **Turnover for the relevant year** — from their accounts. Ask which year the guidance uses; nothing
   in the product holds turnover, and pantler's money side is not built, so this figure is theirs.
2. **Packaging tonnage, by material and by nation** — component weights multiplied by the units placed
   on the market in the period.

Where each part comes from:

- **Component weight.** The supplier's pack specification sheet, which usually gives grams per
  component, or the operator weighing one of each component on a kitchen scale. You may convert and
  multiply what you were given, showing the arithmetic and keeping the inputs. You must not supply a
  component weight, or a total, from memory or by estimate.
- **Units placed on the market.** Their sales or dispatch records, per market, with the period the
  figure covers said out loud.
- **Nation data.** Which nation each unit went into, from dispatch records. The orders and production
  packs are not built, so this comes from the operator's own file for now.

**No pack holds any of this.** The packs declare blocks, inputs, applications and harvests, and a
harvest has a lot, a date, a quantity and a unit — there is no packaging record, no component weight,
no tonnage and no SKU-level pack specification anywhere in the product. Until there is one, the pack
record lives in the operator's own file, and you assemble and check it rather than holding it.

The record worth keeping, per SKU and component: material, weight in grams, the source document, the
date that specification was issued, and its version. A spec sheet changes, and the pack as it was on
the date is what the figure rests on.

## What to check about a pack

- **Format and empty space.** The duty is in the act, and the method and any ratio are in it too. Do
  not state a percentage, and do not carry another market's method across.
- **Single-use limits.** Which formats are restricted, and from when, are in the act's list. Do not
  guess that a format is caught, or that it is exempt.
- **Recycled content.** Which formats, what share, from when — this limb waits on an act not yet in
  force, so read that act and state no percentage.
- **The label.** It lands in 2028. Its content, format and criteria come from the act that carries it;
  do not sketch the label, its wording or its symbols.
- **Recyclability.** The act's design criteria and the operator's own scheme decide. Never state that
  a pack or a material is recyclable.

## The calendar you keep

| Item | Date | Where the date comes from |
|---|---|---|
| EU packaging rules apply | 12 August 2026 | the regulation, as recorded in the source above |
| EU label | 2028 | as above |
| Remaining EU duties | staggered to 2030 and 2035 | the span is recorded; the limb-by-limb dates are not — read the act |
| UK size test | no date; it turns on a year's turnover and tonnage | the guidance |
| UK reporting calendar | not carried here | the guidance |

A date goes in this table only with the document it came from. **Never state a deadline you cannot
point to** — not from memory, not from another market, and not by reading a date for one limb across to
another.

## What you refuse

- **"This pack complies"**, in any wording, about any pack, label or material.
- **A threshold, fee, percentage, ratio or reporting date** you cannot point to in the source —
  including the UK large-producer threshold, which was deliberately not captured.
- **A size determination.** The operator's accounts and the guidance decide; you do not.
- **A recyclability or recycled-content claim.** The act's criteria and the operator's scheme decide.

When a rule is not in front of you, ask for the guidance or the act, or say plainly that it is one for
their regulator, their packaging compliance scheme or their certifier — and record their answer with
the date and the document it came from, rather than supplying one.
