# Composition, and the tells that give a card away

Read this when a card is not landing and you cannot say why. The audit measures what can be measured;
this page is the part that still needs a judgement.

## Composition first

### One idea

A card carries one message. If you have two, you have two cards, or a carousel. A card that says
three things is a card nobody reads.

### One shape

Before colour, decide the shape: a stacked left column; a big word and a rule; a list with a header;
a photograph and a caption block. Write it down in a line ("the headline is the top half, the list
is the bottom half, one rule between"). If you cannot say the shape in a line, you have not chosen
one.

### Hierarchy is scale, weight and space

Not a coloured strip, not a badge, not a shadow, not an icon in a rounded square. Those are the
things a model reaches for when it has no hierarchy, and they are what makes a card look generated.

### Alignment

Everything aligns to one left edge, or to a deliberate second one. Nothing is centred because you
did not decide. Numbers on a price board align right to one edge. Dates sit on the same optical line
on every card in a series.

### Space

Margins are 8–10% of the short side. Space between blocks is larger than space inside a block —
otherwise the card reads as one grey mass. A card with room around it looks considered; a card with
everything pushed to the edges looks cheap.

### Scale

Two or three type sizes, and a big jump between the display and the text. A headline at 1.4× the
body is a mistake; a headline at 3–5× is a decision.

## The tells

Each of these is a point of slop. Diagnose before repairing.

1. **A gradient used as the whole visual idea.** Colour is doing the work composition should do.
2. **An accent chosen because it is the default** — the purple, the teal, the startup blue — rather
   than because it belongs to this business.
3. **Three equal tiles**, with icon, heading and one sentence, nothing prioritised.
4. **A coloured rail down the side** of every block, pretending to be organisation.
5. **Glass, blur or a shadow with no light behind it.**
6. **A giant number filling space** that should carry a sentence.
7. **A rounded-square icon above every heading.**
8. **Everything centred** because no composition was chosen.
9. **The default UI sans** used without a reason, next to a display face it is fighting.
10. **The wrong surface** — a hero on a dashboard, a feature grid on a market poster.
11. **A fake badge or seal**, suggesting a certification the business does not hold.
12. **A photograph standing in for a product photo**, or a stock image of "farmers".

Diagnose, then repair:

- **3, 8 or 10** → re-layout. Recolouring will not fix it.
- **1, 2, 9** → change the palette or the type pairing.
- **4, 5, 6, 7** → remove the decoration and replace it with real hierarchy.
- **11, 12** → remove it. These are honesty failures, not taste failures.

Re-score after the repair. A card that lost its badges and gained nothing reads thin — the fix is
substance (a larger headline, a real photograph, a whole field of colour), not more decoration.

## The five dimensions

Score 0–2 each. Ship at 7 or more.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Idea** | nothing is being said | a category message | something only this business could say |
| **Composition** | accidental | tidy but generic | a shape that carries the idea |
| **Type** | default, fighting itself | one face doing too much | sizes and roles clearly decided |
| **Colour** | default or muddy | one good decision, one weak | every colour earning its place |
| **Finish** | clipped, crowded, placeholders | correct but flat | nothing to fix |

The last point is the one people skip: **finish is not polish**. It is whether a word is clipped,
whether a margin is uneven by 6 px, whether a placeholder survived. The audit catches most of it; the
eye catches the rest.

## What a food business's card usually needs

- **A day and a place**, stated plainly. Market stalls run on "Saturday, 7 to 1".
- **What is ready**, not what is aspirational. Six lines maximum on a feed card.
- **One action** — come, order, call, join the list.
- **A photograph of the actual thing**, or no photograph.
- **No adjectives** the business would not say out loud at the stall.
