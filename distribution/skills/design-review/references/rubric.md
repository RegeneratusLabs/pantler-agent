# The rubric

Five dimensions, 0–2 each. Ship at 7. The point of writing it down is that a review should not change
depending on the reviewer's mood.

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| **0** | actively wrong — a defect, not a taste difference |
| **1** | correct and unconsidered — nothing broken, nothing decided |
| **2** | decided — you could explain the choice to the operator in one line |

## 1. Idea

- **0** — nothing is being said, or three things are saying it at once.
- **1** — a category message: "fresh produce", "quality ingredients", "family owned".
- **2** — something only this business could say: a variety, a day, a place, a way of growing.

## 2. Composition

- **0** — things are placed where they fit. Overlaps, collisions, no margin, one grey mass.
- **1** — tidy but generic: headline, three tiles, a button. The category default.
- **2** — a shape you can describe in a line, and the eye has somewhere to go first, second, third.

## 3. Type

- **0** — clipped, overlapping, or a display face doing body copy's job.
- **1** — one family used at four sizes with no clear reason; or two faces fighting for the same role.
- **2** — two or three sizes, roles decided, tracking deliberate, the display face only where it is
  large enough to work.

## 4. Colour

- **0** — contrast below 4.5:1, the accent is unreadable, or a gradient is doing the composition.
- **1** — one good decision and one weak one: a nice ink, a default accent.
- **2** — ground, ink and one accent, each earning its place; the accent used once, for something.

## 5. Finish

- **0** — a clipped word, a placeholder, a fake badge, an invented price, a 6 px mismatch on a
  repeated edge.
- **1** — correct but flat: all the right parts, none of them adjusted to each other.
- **2** — nothing to fix, and the details agree: the rules land on one optical line, the numbers align
  to one edge, the last line is not a single word.

## What is a defect, and what is taste

**Defect** — must be fixed before hand-over, and is never a matter of opinion:

- text outside the artboard or clipped by its own box;
- contrast below 4.5:1 for body, 3:1 for large display;
- a price, date, name or claim the operator did not give;
- a certification badge the business does not hold;
- a font or image the business cannot licence;
- a placeholder left in the finished thing.

**Taste** — say it once, offer the alternative, then let it go:

- serif or grotesque;
- centred or left-aligned;
- a photograph or a field of colour;
- warm or cool ground.

A review that argues taste as if it were defect is a review that loses the operator.
