# The brand interview, in full

Two waves, and the reason for each question. The rule under all of it: **a blank is a question, not
an invitation to invent.** A business with no colour does not need you to pick one quietly; it needs
one question and a default it can change.

## Wave one — the two that change everything

### 1. How should it feel?

Five postures. Give each with a one-line picture, not an adjective alone:

| Posture | The picture | What it rules in |
|---|---|---|
| Warm and rustic | flour on a bench, a jar with a hand-written label | serif display, cream grounds, terracotta or plum |
| Clean and modern | a shopfront painted last year | one grotesque, one accent, tight grid |
| Bold and market | a board you can read from the footpath | condensed caps, dark or saturated ground |
| Quiet and editorial | a page in a guidebook | measured serif, hairline rules, space |
| Playful and handmade | a paper bag with a stamp on it | mono labels, warm ground, a rule that looks pressed |

If the operator answers with a sixth thing ("like my grandmother's kitchen"), map it to the closest
posture and say which one you are reading it as, so they can correct you.

### 2. Where will people meet it first?

This decides what has to work hardest, and it is the answer most likely to be skipped.

| First meeting | What has to carry |
|---|---|
| A feed | the card at 1080, read in half a second, on a phone |
| A market stall | a sign read at three metres, then a card read in the hand |
| A label | small type that survives print, and a mark that survives 24 px |
| A website | a page read at 1280 and at 390, with a name at the top |

## Wave two — the six that pin it down

Every one is optional. Say so, and say what happens if they skip it.

### 3. A colour they use, or would never use

- **They name one** → that is the accent; the ground is chosen to clear contrast against it.
- **They name one they hate** → add it to `never`, and keep it out of every board.
- **They have none** → "I'll start from this and you can change it." Use the direction's palette.

### 4. A logo file

- **They have one** → place it; do not trace or recolour it. Ask for a PNG or SVG, not a photo of a
  sign.
- **They have none** → "none" is a complete answer. The wordmark is the mark.
- **They have one they are unhappy with** → this is a redesign. Say that a designer should do it and
  offer the wordmark as an interim, clearly labelled.

### 5. How the name is written

As it is, lower case, or with a line under it (a tagline). This is a real decision — it changes the
lockup — and it is theirs, not yours. Never "improve" their capitalisation. If their name is already
all one case, keep it.

### 6. What it should look like it costs

Everyday · honest mid · special. This is not snobbery; it decides the amount of white space, the
weight of the type and how much decoration is allowed. A "special" identity with no space around the
name reads cheap; an "everyday" one with wide margins reads aspirational and wrong.

### 7. Anything that must never appear

Cartoons, gold foil, green leaves, a particular word, a colour a competitor uses. Record it in
`never` and honour it in every board and every later card. This is the cheapest consistency there is.

### 8. Two or three things they like

A shop, a label, an album cover, a link. Read them for a *reason* ("the type is tight and the colour
is one", not "this is nice"), say the reason back in a line, and use it to choose between directions.

## When to stop asking

- **Two waves, then a draft.** If wave two goes unanswered, make the boards from wave one and the
  defaults, and mark every default on the board so they can see what you chose.
- **Never ask a question the tool can answer.** Contrast, type sizes, safe areas and file sizes are
  measured; do not ask the operator about them.
- **Never ask a question the business owns.** Their name, their tagline, their colours, their claims
  and their prices are theirs. Ask, do not fill.

## What to say when they have nothing at all

> "No problem — most businesses start with nothing but a name and a phone photo. I'll set a starter
> identity: a wordmark, a ground, one accent and a type pairing. It'll be consistent across everything
> you make, and when you're ready for a proper identity a designer can build on it rather than start
> again."
