# EUDR due diligence

Written 2026-10-16, from the sources named above. **These rules have already moved twice** — the
application date has changed and the scope act's Annex has been amended — so every date below is the
date it was true on, and a reader should re-check the source before anyone relies on it.

Use this when the operator mentions exporting to the EU, a buyer asking about deforestation or a
due-diligence statement, the coordinates of a block, or a commodity that may be in scope: cattle,
cocoa, coffee, palm, rubber, soy, wood.

## The one rule

**You hold the records; you never clear a consignment.** Do not say a plot, a product, a shipment or a
supplier is deforestation-free, legal or compliant, and do not say goods may enter the EU. That answer
belongs to the operator's customer, their competent authority or their certifier. What you can do is
put the evidence in order, name the document you read each rule from, and say what is missing.

## When the rule bites

Two application dates, from the amending regulation:

| Who | Applies from |
|---|---|
| Large and medium operators, and traders | 30 December 2026 |
| Micro and small undertakings | 30 June 2027 |

Which tier the operator is in is a question for their own adviser. The micro and small test keys off an
establishment date of 31 December 2024, and the size definitions themselves are in the regulation, not
in this skill. Do not decide the tier for them, and do not state a size threshold.

Scope is decided by commodity **and HS code**, in the delegated scope act. The commodity list is short
— the seven above — but the code decides whether a particular product is caught, and that act's Annex
has been amended more than once, so something out of scope a year ago may be in scope now. Never read
scope off a commodity name alone, and never supply an HS code.

## The order of work

1. **What is being sold, to whom, and where it lands.** Ask for the customer, the destination country
   and what the goods are. The obligation is tiered — the act says who in the chain does what — so ask
   who on the EU side is dealing with it rather than assuming the grower lodges anything.
2. **The HS code, from the operator's own paperwork.** The code on the customs entry, or the one their
   broker or freight forwarder gives them. You do not choose a code, narrow it to a chapter, or
   confirm from memory that a code is in the act's Annex. With the code and the current Annex in front
   of you, you may say what the act says about that code, and name the act. **No record type in the
   product holds an HS code** — not a block, not a harvest, not a lot — so it stays on the operator's
   own consignment or entry paperwork, and the record names that document.
3. **The plots.** Every plot the commodity came from, with the geolocation the act requires for it —
   including its form and precision, which are in the act's own text and must be read there.
4. **The due-diligence statement.** What it is, who lodges it and what it must carry come from the act
   or the customer's own guidance. Do not draft a reference number, do not state what a statement must
   contain from memory, and never say that one has been lodged.
5. **The risk assessment.** What was checked, what was found and what was done about it — each dated,
   and each pointing at the document it came from.
6. **Retention.** How long the records must be kept is not carried in this skill. Ask the operator's
   adviser, or read it from the regulation; do not state a period.

## Getting a geolocation, and where it goes

The operator will not have a "geolocation" sitting in a folder. Ask which of these they actually have:
a phone or handheld GPS reading taken standing in the block; a pin dropped in their mapping app; an
export from that app; a survey plan; or, for bought-in material, the coordinates the supplier or the
previous seller passed on. Record what it was, not only the numbers.

One record per plot:

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Plot | the name the operator uses, matched to the block name where the plot is theirs |
| Coordinates | exactly as given, in the form the source gave them — no rounding or reformatting by you |
| Source | the device, app or document the coordinates came from |
| Taken by, and on | who took them, and the date |
| Supplier | the supplier's name and country, where the plot is not the operator's own |

**Nothing in pantler holds a geolocation today.** The packs declare blocks, inputs, applications and
harvests, and a block carries a name and an area in hectares — no coordinates, no plot boundary and no
supplier plot. The production and orders packs are not built. Until a record type exists, this table
lives in the operator's own file: you help assemble it, check it for gaps, and say what is missing
rather than holding it.

## What you refuse

- **A compliance verdict**, in any wording, about any plot, product, supplier or consignment.
- **An HS code**, or any statement about whether a code is in scope, that you cannot point to in the
  current Annex.
- **A size threshold, or a tier decision** — the definitions are in the regulation, not here.
- **A form name, a reference-number format, a precision requirement, a retention period or a fee.**
  None of these is carried here; each is one question to the act, the customer's guidance or the
  operator's adviser.
- **"That commodity is out of scope."** The code decides, and without the code the honest answer is
  that you do not have it.

When a rule is not in front of you, ask for the document — the consolidated regulation and its Annex
as amended, the customer's guidance, or the operator's own certifier — and say what it would settle.

## The date on this page

This was written on 2026-10-16. The two dates above come from the amending regulation, and the scope
from the delegated act; both have moved before. **Never state a deadline you cannot point to.** When

the document it came from and when they told you, and mark it as their date rather than yours.
