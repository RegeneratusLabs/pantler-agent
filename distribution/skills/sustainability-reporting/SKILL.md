# Sustainability reporting a bank, buyer or grant body asks for

Use this when a bank, a lender, a buyer, a supermarket, a grant body or a tender asks the operator for
sustainability information — energy, emissions, waste, water, workforce, policies — or when they
mention VSME, ESRS, a questionnaire, a data request or a reporting deadline.

## The one rule

**You assemble the record; the operator asserts the figure.** Never state that a report, a
questionnaire or a data collection is compliant with anything, and never state an environmental
outcome the records do not support: no "our emissions fell", no "carbon neutral", no "we are
sustainable". A figure with a source is what this skill produces; a claim is not.

## What this is, and what changed

The revised ESRS and the Voluntary Standard were adopted on **21 September 2026** as **(EU)
2026/1560**. The standard is voluntary: it exists so a small business can answer a request. What
creates the duty to answer is the bank's, buyer's or grant body's own ask — so the ask, not the
standard, is the specification worked to here.

The citable text is the **Official Journal**, (EU) 2026/1560. EFRAG's own guidance is where the
interpretation lives, and its reuse terms were **not verified**. Read it, cite the Official Journal,
link out — reproduce nothing from it.

## Start with the request, not the standard

Two things decide everything else, and both come from the counterparty:

- **the period** the report covers, and **which parts of the business** are in it — ask; do not assume
  the last financial year, and do not assume the whole operation;
- **the document they sent** — their template, their module, their datapoint list, the format and the
  deadline.

Then work down their document, line by line. The basic module is the set of datapoints a small
business is asked for, and what it asks for — the list, the order and the definitions attached to each
line — is in the standard as published or in the template the counterparty sent. **Do not reconstruct
that list from memory**, and do not name a datapoint or an annex you cannot read in front of you. If
the operator asks what the module wants and no document is in the conversation, the answer is the
document, not a recital.

## Where the answers live

Whatever the request names, the answer is one of the operator's records or it is nothing. Match each
line to a record; where no record answers it, the honest line is **not recorded**, with a note of who
was asked for it.

| The request reaches for | The record behind it |
|---|---|
| energy and fuel | meter readings, electricity and gas bills, fuel dockets |
| materials and inputs | purchase invoices, delivery dockets, the input and spray records |
| waste and recycling | collection dockets, the waste contractor's statements |
| water | water bills and meter readings |
| transport and deliveries | dispatch records, delivery dockets, vehicle logs |
| workforce | hours, wage records, the contractor list |
| suppliers | the supplier list, and the certificates the operator holds |
| the business's own position | its certificates, its policies, and last year's report |

Two cautions. **The match is the operator's to confirm** — you propose a record, they say whether it
is the right one. And **a record answers for its own period**: do not carry a figure from one year
into another because the request looks similar.

## Arithmetic stays out

Every total, tonnage, kilowatt-hour, emission, ratio, intensity and percentage is computed by a tool,
or is the operator's own calculation recorded as theirs. **You never compute one.** You do not apply a
conversion factor, you do not annualise a part-year figure, and you do not convert a unit to make two
lines agree. A conversion factor is a value with a source: the counterparty's, or the operator's, with
its date.

## Thresholds and judgements you must not supply

- **Materiality.** Whether a topic is material, and the threshold at which it must be reported, comes
  from the counterparty's document or the standard as published. No figure for it is in this skill, and
  none is in your memory either.
- **Definitions.** What counts as the business, as an employee, as a site, as an emission — from the
  source.
- **Assurance.** Whether anything must be verified, and by whom, is in the ask.

When a question turns on one of these, ask for the source and record the date it was read.

## What to record

- the request: who asked, what they sent, the period, the format, the deadline;
- each figure with its source document and the date it was read, and whether a tool or the operator
  produced it;
- what is missing, who was asked for it, when, and what came back;
- the version of the reply that went out, and who signed it off.

This record is what makes next year's request cheap. A figure whose source is not written down has to
be found again.

## What to refuse

- **A compliance statement.** Never "this meets the standard", "this is VSME-compliant", "this is
  audit-ready".
- **A number from memory.** No industry average, no "a business your size is usually about", no figure
  carried over from another operator's report.
- **An estimate dressed as a figure.** If the operator estimates, it is recorded as their estimate,
  with its basis and its date, and it stays visibly theirs.
- **A claim the records do not support.** The records support "we used this much", not "we are
  sustainable".
- **EFRAG's guidance text.** Its reuse terms were not verified: cite the Official Journal and link out.
